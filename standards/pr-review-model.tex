\section{Purpose and scope}

Eight empirical fix rounds on the review-state rules each surfaced the next
defect. This model replaces those rounds. It states what the leader reads from
GitHub, the events that change it, and the guard $merge\_gate$ puts on
$Merge$. ProB checks two things. No reachable state merges a commit no review
covered or an unanswered finding, or answers a finding while its fix awaits
local review or push.
Every non-merged state can still move, so the only waits are on the operator,
the author, or a bot.

The named assumptions A1--A9 in the file header bound what the result covers.

\section{Two machines}

One machine with both sides has a state space ProB cannot hold: at 2 commits,
1 finding, and 1 run it is 899,610 states, and 3 commits do not finish in
30 GB. The review side and the Bugbot side share only $round$, $skipped$, and
$merged$, and each reads the other only through the gate. So each is checked
alone, with the other side replaced by an abstraction that allows at least
every behaviour the real side has.

\textbf{The interface.} The Bugbot side contributes two predicates to the
review side:
\begin{itemize}
\item $bugbot\_in(round)$, a conjunct of $\lnot pending$;
\item $bugbot\_clear$: no live run without a ``proceed'' ruling and no open
  run escalation, a conjunct of $merge\_gate$.
\end{itemize}
The review side contributes $round$ (round 2 opens only through
$OpenRound2$) and the moment of $Merge$.

\textbf{Machine A} (this file) replaces Bugbot with $bbIn$ and $bbClear$.
Each becomes true through a one-shot event and then stays true. The review
side reads both only positively, in guards, so a monotone true enables at least
every review step the real Bugbot side enables: each real trace, projected onto
the review variables, is a trace of machine A with the abstract events
inserted where the real predicate first held.

\textbf{Machine B} (\texttt{pr-review-model-bugbot.tex}) replaces the review
side with two events: round 2 may open whenever Bugbot is in for round 1, and
$Merge$ may fire whenever the Bugbot conjuncts hold. Both are weaker than the
real guards, so each real trace projected onto the Bugbot variables is a trace
of machine B.

\textbf{Why the split is sound.} Each invariant mentions the variables of one
machine plus the shared $round$, $skipped$, and $merged$. It holds on every
real trace, because the trace's projection is a trace of the machine that owns
the invariant.

Deadlock freedom rests on a lemma, stated, proved by cases, and checked as I6
in machine B: in every non-merged state where none of B's own events is
enabled ($BugbotStart$ aside, which the proof does not need), the Bugbot
conjuncts hold. B's $OpenRound2$ and $Merge$ are not among B's own events:
they stand for the review side, so B being deadlock-free says nothing on its
own, and the argument does not use it. Take a real non-merged state where
nothing can move. B's own events are real events with the same guards, so all
are disabled, and by the lemma $bugbot\_clear$ and $bugbot\_in(round)$ hold
(the latter unless the PR is skipped, where $\lnot pending$ does not read it).
Project the trace onto machine A with $BugbotInArrives$ for the current round
and $BugbotClears$ inserted just before this state where they have not yet
fired. $BugbotInArrives$ is inserted on a skipped PR too, harmlessly:
$NotPending$ reads $bbIn$ only when $skipped = no$. Neither abstract event is
enabled in the projected state, since both have fired. Every other variable is
the real one, and A reads $bbIn$ and $bbClear$ only positively. Machine A has
no deadlock, so some A event is enabled in the projected state. Each such
event is enabled in the real state too, implies another A event that is, or
is held only by an A2 conjunct, whose own event (a Copilot review or its 30
minutes, CI completing, the demo) then moves. Besides the A2 conjuncts, three
A guards are weaker than the real ones:
\begin{itemize}
\item $EscalateFinding$ takes any unanswered finding, not only a new High.
  $AnswerLedger$ has the same finding guard: the real ledger answer covers a
  finding that is not a new High, the real escalation one that is.
\item $FallbackStart$ may fire before 30 minutes (A1): the real ``30 minutes
  pass'' event moves.
\item $OpenRound2$ omits ``a High was fixed''. With $bbClear = yes$, $Merge$
  is enabled, or one of these is: $RuleDismiss$ ($escF \neq \emptyset$),
  $FixBlocked$ ($fixing \cup fixPending \neq \emptyset$), $AnswerLedger$,
  $DeltaReviewCompletes$ ($deltaRun \neq \emptyset$), $DeltaReviewStart$
  ($head \notin deltaHead$), or $Promote$ ($head \in deltaHead$, every finding
  answered).
\end{itemize}
So the real state can move, and the real system has no deadlock.

\section{Given sets and enumerations}

Commits and findings are opaque identities.

\begin{zed}
  [COMMIT, FINDING]
\end{zed}

\begin{zed}
  Flag ::= yes | no
\end{zed}

\begin{zed}
  ROUND == 1 \upto 3
\end{zed}

\section{State}

\textbf{Commits.} $anc$ is ancestry, reflexive: $a \mapsto b \in anc$ when $a$
is $b$ or an ancestor of $b$. A push extends it; a rebase starts a fresh
history, so the old commits are no longer ancestors of the head. $used$ keeps
commit identities from being reused (A4).

\begin{schema}{Commits}
  head : COMMIT \\
  used : \power COMMIT \\
  anc : COMMIT \rel COMMIT
\where
  head \in used \\
  head \mapsto head \in anc \\
  \dom anc \subseteq used \\
  \ran anc \subseteq used
\end{schema}

\textbf{Review markers.} $reviewedHead$ and $deltaHead$ are optional commits,
sets of size 0 or 1. $deltaRun$ is the head a local delta review was started
on and has not yet completed; $deltaRange$ the commits it covers; $deltaF$ the
findings the last completed delta review raised. $skipped$ is a bot-authored
PR. $trigHead$ is $head(N)$, the head when the current round was triggered.
$seen$ and $overlap$ are ghost variables, read by no guard: $seen$ is every
commit a completed review covered, and $overlap$ records that a delta review
re-covered a commit already seen.

\begin{schema}{Review}
  reviewedHead, deltaHead, deltaRun, trigHead : \power COMMIT \\
  deltaRange, seen : \power COMMIT \\
  deltaF : \power FINDING \\
  overlap, skipped : Flag \\
  round : ROUND
\where
  \# reviewedHead \leq 1 \\
  \# deltaHead \leq 1 \\
  \# deltaRun \leq 1 \\
  \# trigHead = 1
\end{schema}

\textbf{Qodo and its outage fallback.} $qodoRun$ and $fbRun$ map each round
with a session or fallback in flight to the commit it started on. Sessions of
different rounds may overlap: a round-2 session may start while a late
round-1 session still runs, and so may two fallbacks. The head may move while
any of them runs.

\begin{schema}{Qodo}
  qodoDone, qodoStarted, fbStarted : \power ROUND \\
  qodoRun, fbRun : ROUND \pfun COMMIT
\end{schema}

\textbf{The Bugbot interface.} $bbIn$ is the rounds whose $bugbot\_in$ the
Bugbot side has reported; $bbClear$ reports $bugbot\_clear$. Both are
monotone (section ``Two machines'').

\begin{schema}{BugbotIface}
  bbIn : \power ROUND \\
  bbClear : Flag
\end{schema}

\textbf{Findings and escalations.} Any number of findings may be escalated at
once ($escF$). $fixPending$ holds findings the operator ruled ``fix'' whose fix
has not yet landed; $fixing$ holds unruled findings whose fix commit awaits
local review. Four ghosts: $fixRuledEver$, $pushFixed$ (findings answered by
a fix push), $dismissedEver$ (findings answered by a ``dismiss'' ruling), and
$ledgered$ (findings answered by a ledger line or a Qodo CLI dismiss).

\begin{schema}{Issues}
  findings, answered, escF, fixPending, fixing : \power FINDING \\
  fixRuledEver, pushFixed, dismissedEver, ledgered : \power FINDING
\where
  answered \subseteq findings \\
  escF \subseteq findings \setminus answered \\
  fixPending \subseteq findings \\
  escF \cap fixPending = \emptyset
\end{schema}

\begin{schema}{Gate}
  merged : Flag
\end{schema}

\begin{schema}{State}
  Commits \\
  Review \\
  Qodo \\
  BugbotIface \\
  Issues \\
  Gate
\end{schema}

\textbf{The invariant.} Every reachable state must satisfy these; ProB checks
them in every state it visits. They live in $Invariant$, not in $State$: a
predicate of $State$ is part of every $\Delta State$, so it would silently
disable a violating operation instead of reporting it.
(I1) A merge leaves every commit in the head's history covered by a review
and every finding answered. (Machine B states the Bugbot half of I1.)
(I2) Every commit in a reviewed marker's history was covered by a completed
review.
(I3) No delta review re-covers a commit an earlier review already covered.
(I5) A finding ruled ``fix'' is answered only by a fix push, or by a later
``dismiss'' ruling after its fix was blocked.
(I7) Every answer has one recorded path (a pushed fix, a ``dismiss'' ruling,
or a ledger line), and no finding is answered while its fix awaits local
review or push. $fixPending \cap answered = \emptyset$ lives here, not in
$Issues$, so a violating operation is reported, not disabled.
(I4, the Bugbot fallback, and I6, the deadlock lemma, are machine B's.)

\begin{schema}{Invariant}
  State
\where
  merged = yes \implies \\
  \quad~ ( \{ a : COMMIT | a \mapsto head \in anc \} \subseteq seen \land \\
  \quad~\quad~ findings \subseteq answered ) \\
  ( \forall m : reviewedHead @ \{ a : COMMIT | a \mapsto m \in anc \} \subseteq seen ) \\
  overlap = no \\
  fixRuledEver \cap answered \subseteq pushFixed \cup dismissedEver \\
  answered \subseteq pushFixed \cup dismissedEver \cup ledgered \\
  answered \cap fixing = \emptyset \\
  answered \cap fixPending = \emptyset
\end{schema}

\section{Derived terms}

$\lnot pending$: a skipped PR waits on no bot review; any other PR needs Qodo
and Bugbot in for the current round.

\begin{schema}{NotPending}
  State
\where
  skipped = yes \lor ( round \in qodoDone \land round \in bbIn )
\end{schema}

$reviewed\_head$ never moves backward. A completing review moves it to the
commit it ran on only when the current $reviewed\_head$ is unset, is off the
head's history (a rebase orphaned it), or is an ancestor of that commit. A
late session that ran on an older or orphaned commit leaves a marker that
already covers the head alone. $n?$ is the round of the completing review.

\begin{schema}{Advances}
  State \\
  n? : ROUND
\where
  \forall m : reviewedHead @ m \mapsto head \in anc \implies \\
  \quad~ ( \exists q : COMMIT @ n? \mapsto q \in qodoRun \land m \mapsto q \in anc )
\end{schema}

\begin{schema}{FbAdvances}
  State \\
  n? : ROUND
\where
  \forall m : reviewedHead @ m \mapsto head \in anc \implies \\
  \quad~ ( \exists q : COMMIT @ n? \mapsto q \in fbRun \land m \mapsto q \in anc )
\end{schema}

\section{Branch operations}

$Push$ adds a commit on top of the head.

\begin{schema}{Push}
  \Delta State \\
  \Xi Review \\
  \Xi Qodo \\
  \Xi BugbotIface \\
  \Xi Issues \\
  \Xi Gate \\
  c? : COMMIT
\where
  merged = no \\
  c? \notin used \\
  head' = c? \\
  used' = used \cup \{ c? \} \\
  anc' = anc \cup ( \{ a : COMMIT | a \mapsto head \in anc \} \cross \{ c? \} ) \cup \{ c? \mapsto c? \}
\end{schema}

$Rebase$ replaces the history: the new head has no old ancestor, so neither
marker is an ancestor of it. Its content keeps every fix (A7).

\begin{schema}{Rebase}
  \Delta State \\
  \Xi Review \\
  \Xi Qodo \\
  \Xi BugbotIface \\
  \Xi Issues \\
  \Xi Gate \\
  c? : COMMIT
\where
  merged = no \\
  c? \notin used \\
  head' = c? \\
  used' = used \cup \{ c? \} \\
  anc' = anc \cup \{ c? \mapsto c? \}
\end{schema}

\section{Qodo and the outage fallback}

\begin{schema}{QodoStart}

  \Xi Commits \\
  \Xi Review \\
  \Xi BugbotIface \\
  \Xi Issues \\
  \Xi Gate
\where
  merged = no \\
  skipped = no \\

  qodoDone' = qodoDone \\
  fbStarted' = fbStarted \\
  fbRun' = fbRun
\end{schema}

  fbStarted' = fbStarted \\

  deltaRun' = deltaRun \\
  deltaRange' = deltaRange \\
  deltaF' = deltaF \\
  overlap' = overlap \\
  skipped' = skipped \\

\section{Findings}

  \Xi Commits \\
  \Xi Review \\
  \Xi Qodo \\
  \Xi BugbotIface \\
  \Xi Gate \\
  f? : FINDING
\where
  merged = no \\
  f? \notin findings \\
  findings' = findings \cup \{ f? \} \\
  answered' = answered \\
  escF' = escF \\
  fixPending' = fixPending \\
  fixing' = fixing \\
  fixRuledEver' = fixRuledEver \\
  pushFixed' = pushFixed \\
  dismissedEver' = dismissedEver \\
  ledgered' = ledgered
\end{schema}

The leader answers a finding by a ledger line or a Qodo CLI dismiss. A finding
under escalation, under a ``fix'' ruling, or with a fix in local review cannot
take this path.

\begin{schema}{AnswerLedger}
  \Delta State \\
  \Xi Commits \\
  \Xi Review \\
  \Xi Qodo \\
  \Xi BugbotIface \\
  \Xi Gate \\
  f? : FINDING
\where
  merged = no \\
  f? \in findings \setminus ( answered \cup escF \cup fixPending \cup fixing ) \\
  answered' = answered \cup \{ f? \} \\
  ledgered' = ledgered \cup \{ f? \} \\
  findings' = findings \\
  escF' = escF \\
  fixPending' = fixPending \\
  fixing' = fixing \\
  fixRuledEver' = fixRuledEver \\
  pushFixed' = pushFixed \\
  dismissedEver' = dismissedEver
\end{schema}

The leader fixes an unruled finding on its own authority: the fix commit
exists and awaits local review. It answers nothing yet, whatever the source.

\begin{schema}{FixStart}
  \Delta State \\
  \Xi Commits \\
  \Xi Review \\
  \Xi Qodo \\

\section{The local delta review}

The delta review starts when nothing is pending and the head is covered by
neither marker. The guard does not ask whether the head is ``newer'' than a
marker: an unset $deltaHead$ (defect (1)) and a marker a rebase orphaned
(defect (4)) both leave the head uncovered. $delta\_base$ is the markers that
are the head's ancestors, $deltaHead$ as well as $reviewedHead$ (defect (3)).
The range is every commit in the head's history that no $delta\_base$ commit
reaches; with no $delta\_base$ it is the whole history. A bot-authored PR takes
this path with no marker at all.

\begin{schema}{DeltaReviewStart}
  \Delta State \\
  \Xi Commits \\
  \Xi Qodo \\
  \Xi BugbotIface \\
  \Xi Issues \\
  \Xi Gate
\where
  merged = no \\
  NotPending \\
  deltaRun = \emptyset \\
  head \notin reviewedHead \cup deltaHead \\
  deltaRun' = \{ head \} \\
  deltaRange' = \{ a : COMMIT | a \mapsto head \in anc \} \setminus \\
  \quad~ \{ a : COMMIT | ( \exists m : reviewedHead \cup deltaHead @ \\
  \quad~\quad~ m \mapsto head \in anc \land a \mapsto m \in anc ) \} \\
  ( deltaRange' \cap seen = \emptyset \implies overlap' = overlap ) \\
  ( deltaRange' \cap seen \neq \emptyset \implies overlap' = yes ) \\
  reviewedHead' = reviewedHead \\
  deltaHead' = deltaHead \\
  deltaF' = deltaF \\
  seen' = seen \\
  round' = round \\
  skipped' = skipped \\
  trigHead' = trigHead
\end{schema}

The delta review completes. Only now is $deltaHead$ set; its findings are
findings like any other.

\begin{schema}{DeltaReviewCompletes}
  \Delta State \\
  \Xi Commits \\
  \Xi Qodo \\
  \Xi BugbotIface \\
  \Xi Gate \\
  new? : \power FINDING
\where
  merged = no \\
  deltaRun \neq \emptyset \\
  new? \cap findings = \emptyset \\
  \# new? \leq 1 \\
  deltaHead' = deltaRun \\
  deltaRun' = \emptyset \\
  seen' = seen \cup deltaRange \\
  deltaRange' = \emptyset \\
  deltaF' = new? \\
  findings' = findings \cup new? \\
  reviewedHead' = reviewedHead \\
  overlap' = overlap \\
  round' = round \\
  skipped' = skipped \\
  trigHead' = trigHead \\
  answered' = answered \\
  escF' = escF \\
  fixPending' = fixPending \\
  fixing' = fixing \\
  fixRuledEver' = fixRuledEver \\
  pushFixed' = pushFixed \\
  dismissedEver' = dismissedEver \\
  ledgered' = ledgered
\end{schema}

When a completed delta review covers the head and its findings are answered,
$reviewedHead$ moves to it. It reads $deltaHead$, which only a completed review
sets, never the review in flight (defect (5)).

  deltaHead' = deltaHead \\
  deltaRun' = deltaRun \\
  deltaRange' = deltaRange \\
  deltaF' = deltaF \\
  seen' = seen \\
  overlap' = overlap \\
  round' = round \\
  skipped' = skipped \\
  trigHead' = trigHead
\end{schema}

\section{Merge}

$merge\_gate$: nothing pending, Bugbot clear, the head reviewed, every finding
answered, at most two rounds, no open finding escalation. Machine B checks the
Bugbot conjuncts.

\begin{schema}{Merge}
  \Delta State \\
  \Xi Commits \\
  \Xi Review \\
  \Xi Qodo \\
  \Xi BugbotIface \\
  \Xi Issues
\where
  merged = no \\
  NotPending \\
  bbClear = yes \\
  head \in reviewedHead \\
  findings \subseteq answered \\
  round \leq 2 \\
  escF = \emptyset \\
  merged' = yes
\end{schema}

After the merge nothing changes. This stutter keeps the merged state from
reading as a deadlock.

\begin{schema}{Merged}
  \Xi State
\where
  merged = yes
\end{schema}
